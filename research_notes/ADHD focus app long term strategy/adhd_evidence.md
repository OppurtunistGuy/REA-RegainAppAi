# Evidence Brief: Digital Interventions for Focus, Task Initiation and Learning in ADHD and Distractible Learners (as of Nov 2026)

Evidence-level legend used below: **[MA]** meta-analysis/systematic review, **[RCT]** randomized trial, **[FIELD]** field/in-the-wild study, **[LAB]** small lab study, **[PILOT]** uncontrolled/feasibility, **[ANEC]** anecdote/opinion/vendor. **COI** = industry-funded or developer-authored.

## 1. Friction/pause interventions, hard blocking, and commitment devices on smartphone use

### Takeaway
This is one of the better-evidenced areas for general users (not ADHD-specific). Short friction plus an easy "dismiss / don't open" option reduces app opens. Of the three components, the dismiss option did the most work, the delay helped, and the reflective message did nothing. Commitment-style limits that cannot be instantly overridden also reduce use, and people show demand for them. No study isolates ADHD users.

### Cited Findings
- **[FIELD+experiment, COI] Grüning, Riedel & Lorenz-Spreen, PNAS 120(8), Feb 2023**, "Directing smartphone use through the self-nudge app one sec." The app adds a roughly 10 s delay, shows a deliberation message, and offers a dismiss option. Over 6 weeks (n = 280), users closed the target app in 36% of open attempts, and open attempts fell 37% versus week 1. The controlled experiment found the **dismiss option was the strongest driver**, the **delay also reduced use**, and the **deliberation message had no effect**. Co-author Riedel is the one sec developer (COI). — [PMC9974409](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409); [Heidelberg record](https://heibib.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/search/Record/1868867595)
- The widely quoted "57% fewer app openings after 6 weeks" figure appears on the company's site. Treat it as a developer claim. — [one sec research page](https://one-sec.app/research/)
- **[FIELD, COI] Haliburton, Grüning et al., CHI 2024**, "A Longitudinal In-the-Wild Investigation of Design Frictions to Prevent Smartphone Overuse." In 1,039 organic users, frictions reduced open attempts over time. I could not load the full text (DNS failure), so effect sizes and habituation data are unverified. — [ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Luke-Haliburton/publication/377774169_A_Longitudinal_In-the-Wild_Investigation_of_Design_Frictions_to_Prevent_Smartphone_Overuse/links/65b7d1311bed776ae313b600/A-Longitudinal-In-the-Wild-Investigation-of-Design-Frictions-to-Prevent-Smartphone-Overuse.pdf)
- **[RCT, large] Allcott, Gentzkow & Song, "Digital Addiction"** (NBER w28936; published in AER 2022). About 2,000 US Android adults installed the purpose-built Phone Dashboard app. Daily per-app limits took effect the next day and could only be "snoozed" after a delay, so unlike iOS Screen Time they could not be immediately overridden. The limits substantially reduced use, and participants were willing to pay for the commitment device. The authors' model attributes **about 31% of social media use to self-control problems**. That figure is a model estimate, not a direct measurement. — [NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28936/w28936.pdf); [J-PAL copy](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/media/file-research-paper/digital-addiction); [Brookings 2026 version](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/20260602_THP_Song_SocialMedia_Paper.pdf)
- **[FIELD] Pocket Points (rewards for staying off the phone in class).** A university field experiment reported better classroom focus and attendance. It is a draft paper. — [UC Davis draft](https://faculty.econ.ucdavis.edu/faculty/scarrell/phone.pdf)

### Inferences
- The design pattern with the best support is a **short delay plus a prominent, easy "close / do something else" exit**. Long reflective prompts are not supported, because the one sec message component was null.
- Limits that are **pre-committed (take effect later) and cost a delay to override** beat easily dismissed limits. This matches the delay-aversion profile in ADHD: a delay is aversive, so it acts as a real cost. It may also mean ADHD users disable tools that feel too punitive. This last point is untested.
- Hard, non-overridable blocking has weaker direct evidence than "costly-to-override" limits. The Allcott design kept flexibility through snoozes.

### Gaps
- No friction or blocking trial in a diagnosed ADHD sample was found.
- Long-term habituation to friction (does the 10 s delay become automatic tapping?) was not verified, because the CHI 2024 full text was unreachable.
- Exact Allcott effect sizes for the limit arm, recalled as roughly 16 min/day, were not verified in this session.
- No data was found on uninstall or abandonment rates for blocking apps.

## 2. Body doubling, implementation intentions, task chunking, time cues, timeboxing/Pomodoro, reward schedules

### Takeaway
The best-grounded mechanisms are **implementation intentions** (lab evidence in ADHD children), **immediate, frequent reinforcement** (meta-analytic evidence of delay aversion and steeper discounting), and **external time representation**, which is justified by meta-analytic evidence of timing deficits but has few intervention trials. **Body doubling** is popular but has almost no controlled evidence. **Pomodoro** has mixed evidence even in general students and none specific to ADHD.

### Cited Findings
- **Implementation intentions [LAB, single group].**
  - Gawrilow & Gollwitzer (2008, *Cognitive Therapy and Research* 32:261–280) found that ADHD children using if-then plans reached non-ADHD-level inhibition on a Go/No-Go task. Plans combined with stimulant medication gave the best performance. — [Konstanz PDF](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/sites/default/files/08_Gawrilow_Gollwitzer_Implementation_intentions_facilit.pdf)
  - A 2011 follow-up (*J Soc Clin Psychol*) found fewer perseverative errors on a set-shifting task, and if-then plans also helped working-memory math problems with distraction. — [PDF](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/sites/default/files/Gawrilow_Gollwitzer_Oettingen_Executive_Function_JSCP_2011.pdf)
  - A 2011 study (*Cognitive Therapy and Research*) found that if-then plans improved delay of gratification in ADHD children. — [PDF](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/sites/default/files/Gawrilow_Gollwitzer_Oettingen_Delay_2011-2.pdf)
  - A 2012 study tested mental contrasting combined with implementation intentions (MCII) in ADHD. — [PDF](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/sites/default/files/Gawrilow_et_al_2012_Mental_contrasting_with_implementation.pdf)
  - Caveat: these are small lab studies from one research group, with no independent replication found.
- **Delay aversion / reward [MA].**
  - Sonuga-Barke and colleagues (2021) pooled 37 group comparisons (3,763 participants, 53% ADHD). People with ADHD more often choose small immediate rewards over larger delayed ones, with small-to-medium effects. The effect is amplified when rewards are hypothetical rather than real. — [Southampton eprint](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/420008)
  - A 2016 meta-analysis covered 25 case-control comparisons of monetary delay discounting. — [PMC5049699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5049699)
  - A 2021 Frontiers meta-analysis on decision-making found r ≈ 0.34 for the combined presentation, with altered reward sensitivity and aberrant reinforcement learning. A model-based meta-analysis reported d = 0.43 for temporal discounting. — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2021.519840/pdf)
- **Time blindness [MA].**
  - Marx et al. (2021, *JAACAP*; 55 studies) found meta-analytic evidence of a broad range of timing deficits in ADHD. Estimation and production errors suggest an "accelerated internal clock," and reproduction deficits have attentional and motivational components. — [em-consulte abstract](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1527936/meta-analysis-altered-perceptual-timing-abilities-); [Southampton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/456003)
  - Zheng et al. (children; 27 studies after 2000) found the deficit in time reproduction, measured as absolute discrepancy, but not in time estimation. — [HKU PDF](https://hub.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/309307/1/content.pdf?accept=1)
  - The adult evidence is sparse and inconsistent according to a 2023 review. — [PMC9962130](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9962130/)
- **Body doubling [PILOT/ANEC].**
  - A 2025 VR body-doubling paper (n = 12 adults with ADHD; alone vs. human vs. AI double) states that no peer-reviewed controlled trials exist in adults with ADHD. It notes that one prior ADHD study found no significant effect, and that some neurodivergent users are uncomfortable with social presence. — [arXiv 2509.12153](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12153v1)
  - A 2026 roadmap describes HCI body-doubling research as very recent and limited. — [arXiv 2605.07851](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07851)
  - CHADD covers body doubling as a practitioner strategy, not as trial evidence. — [CHADD](https://chadd.org/attention-article/the-power-of-body-doubling/)
  - Healthline's claim that "a 2024 study found it effective" could not be traced to a study. — [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/adhd/body-double-adhd)
- **Pomodoro / timeboxing [small RCT/field, general students].**
  - Smits, Wenzel & de Bruin (2025, *Behavioral Sciences*) compared Pomodoro, Flowtime and self-regulated breaks. Pomodoro produced faster fatigue increases, and both Pomodoro and Flowtime produced faster motivation decreases, but overall outcomes did not differ. — [PMC12292963](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12292963/)
  - A 2023 *Br J Educ Psychol* study found that fixed, systematic breaks gave mood and efficiency benefits over self-regulated breaks. — [PubMed 36859717](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36859717/)
  - Another 2025 study found self-regulated breaks gave better mood, concentration and motivation, and less distraction. — [MDPI 16/7/1158](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/16/7/1158)
- **Task chunking / compensatory skills [MA, CBT].**
  - Adult ADHD CBT, whose core content is planning, organization and breaking tasks down, outperformed waitlist (SMD 0.76) and active controls (SMD 0.43). — [Cochrane 29566425](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29566425/)
  - Another analysis reported investigator-rated effects of 1.03, 0.66 and 0.32 against waitlist, TAU and active control. These figures show how much waitlist controls inflate effects. — [PubMed 36794797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36794797/)
  - A 32-study meta-analysis found adult CBT effects comparable to child behavioral treatment. Targeted practice of specific compensatory strategies looks like a key active ingredient. — [PubMed 28504540](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28504540/)
  - No meta-analysis isolates chunking as a standalone digital feature.

### Inferences
- **Evidence-aligned features:** guided if-then plan creation ("If it's 9:00 and I open the laptop, then I open the doc first"), immediate small rewards or feedback at task start rather than delayed big rewards, and **visible, analog-style time remaining** (progress rings, countdowns) in place of abstract clock times. The time features are justified by the deficit literature, not by app trials.
- **Pomodoro should be optional and flexible** (adjustable interval, "keep going" when in flow). A rigid 25/5 timer has no ADHD evidence, and in one study it raised fatigue.
- **Body doubling** is low-risk and plausibly useful for task initiation, but should be marketed as "community/co-working," not "proven." Some users find social presence aversive, so make it opt-in.

### Gaps
- No RCT tests external time cues or visual timers in ADHD.
- No ADHD-specific Pomodoro or timeboxing trial exists.
- Task chunking has not been tested as an isolated app feature.
- The 2025 EEG body-doubling study (ASSETS '25, n = 26), reported in secondary sources, could not be verified.

## 3. Gamification and streaks: help or harm? Retention data

### Takeaway
No controlled study compares streak and non-streak designs in ADHD users. That evidence gap is itself a finding. Engagement with mental health and ADHD apps decays steeply, and industry claims of "gamification boosts retention by X%" for ADHD apps are largely unverifiable. The shame and abandonment concern after a broken streak is a plausible, widely voiced argument, but it is untested.

### Cited Findings
- **[Panel data] Baumel et al., JMIR 2019** (93 Android mental health apps): median 15-day retention was 3.9% and 30-day retention 3.3%. The median share of daily active users was 4.0%. Tracker apps (median 6.1%) and mindfulness apps (4.7%) retained better than breathing apps (0%). — [JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567); [PMC6785720](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6785720)
- **[PILOT, COI] Inflow open study, PLOS Digital Health 2022:** 240 adults enrolled, 95 still assessed at 7 weeks (roughly 40%). Median use was 3.86 times per week. Two authors are Inflow co-founders. — [PMC9931323](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9931323)
- **[RCT] FOCUS ADHD app (adults, NCT05551689):** engagement decreased significantly over time. A financial discount group engaged more than the app-only group. — [ClinicalTrials.gov protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/89/NCT05551689/Prot_000.pdf)
- **[RCT] eQuoo gamified mental health app (2020, PLoS ONE):** this trial tested gamification as an attrition reducer. It notes that baseline attrition for mental health apps ranges from 9.6% to 50%. — [PMC7467300](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7467300/)
- **[Review] About 77% of users stop using an app within 3 days of installation.** The same review suggests gamification and personalization may help. — [Tandfonline SAR](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.2147/SAR.S560350)
- **[ANEC]** Claims that ADHD users are harmed by streak resets, through shame and extrinsic-motivation collapse, appear on vendor and dev blogs only. — [Klarity](https://www.helloklarity.com/post/breaking-the-chain-why-streak-features-fail-adhd-users-and-how-to-design-better-alternatives); [DEV](https://dev.to/nucleusos/adhd-support-app-no-streaks-why-gamification-hurts-more-than-it-helps-1ae0)
- A frequently cited "2022 JMIR Serious Games: 48% higher retention" statistic could not be traced to a real source. — [Imaginovation blog](https://imaginovation.net/blog/gamification-adhd-apps-user-retention/)
- **[Qualitative, 2026]** An r/ADHD community study on time management in digital environments is a user-perspective source. — [arXiv 2610.12629](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12629)

### Inferences
- Plan for 30-day retention in the single digits as the base rate. Any retention claim above that needs your own cohort data.
- The delay-aversion and novelty-seeking profile predicts that ADHD users respond strongly to immediate rewards and novelty, and that both effects decay. Rotate rewards, use variable or novel feedback, and avoid single-point-of-failure mechanics. One missed day should not erase progress, so use "streak freezes," rolling counts ("5 of the last 7 days") and comeback rewards.
- Tangible incentives (the FOCUS discount arm, Pocket Points) increased engagement in trials. This is stronger evidence than points or badges.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head trial of streaks against forgiving or no-streak designs in ADHD.
- No published long-term (6–12 month) retention data for any ADHD consumer app.
- The primary publication and retention figures for Inflow's RCT (n = 154, waitlist control, reported by Simply Psychology) could not be verified. — [Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/?p=97474)

## 4. Learning science for distractible learners: spaced repetition, retrieval practice, microlearning, interleaving

### Takeaway
Retrieval practice benefits ADHD students about as much as peers, but it **does not close the gap caused by weak encoding** in unmedicated learners. Spacing is strongly supported in general learners but has no ADHD-specific test. Microlearning and interleaving have no ADHD-specific evidence that I could find.

### Cited Findings
- **[LAB] Minear et al. 2023 (Frontiers in Psychology).** All groups benefited from retrieval practice over restudy, and ADHD status did not moderate the effect. Unmedicated ADHD students performed worse overall, including at encoding. The authors attribute this to less effortful learning strategies and conclude that "improved strategy use may be necessary." — [PMC10397724](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10397724/)
- **[LAB] Dudukovic et al. 2015** found **no testing benefit** for ADHD students on free recall of prose passages. The literature is therefore mixed, with three studies reporting conflicting results. — cited in [Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1186566/full)
- **[LAB] Stern & Halamish 2023** recommend whole-text free recall for ADHD students, combined with strategies that improve the mental organization of the material. — [Frontiers 1186566](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1186566/full)
- Learners with poorer strategies may gain more from retrieval practice. Distributed practice is superior to massed practice in general. — [Frontiers 1301726](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1301726/pdf); spacing algorithm example: [DAS3H, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.06873)

### Inferences
- Build in **active retrieval** (quizzes, free recall) and **scheduled spacing**. Pair them with **encoding scaffolds** (structure, outlines, chunked organization), because retrieval alone does not fix shallow encoding in ADHD.
- "Microlearning" is defensible as a delivery format: short sessions match delay aversion and lower the initiation barrier. Its benefit for ADHD is inferred, not shown.

### Gaps
- No ADHD-specific studies of spacing, interleaving or microlearning were found.
- Existing samples are small and mostly college students.

## 5. Short-form video and attention: established vs. speculative

### Takeaway
A large 2025 meta-analysis establishes a **moderate correlation** between heavier or more compulsive short-form video use and poorer attention and inhibitory control. Causation is not established. The data are mostly cross-sectional, the attention estimate rests on a small, geographically narrow base, and compulsive use matters more than time spent.

### Cited Findings
- **[MA] Nguyen et al. 2025, *Psychological Bulletin* 151(9):1125–1146,** "Feeds, Feelings, and Focus" (71 studies, n = 98,299):
  - cognition overall r = −.34, inhibitory control r = −.41, attention r = −.38;
  - links with anxiety, depression and stress, but no significant link with body image or self-esteem.

  — [NYU IFP abstract](https://ifp.nyu.edu/2025/journal-article-abstracts/bul0000498/); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/large-meta-analysis-links-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-to-poorer-cognitive-and-mental-health/)
- **Caveats:** most included studies are cross-sectional. One outlet reports that the attention analysis relied on only 14 studies, all from China. The effects were tied to "addictive" use and emotional attachment more than to viewing time. — [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/s-is-all-that-scrolling-really-dulling-your-attention-researchers-combed-71-studies-the-link-is-real-but-how-compulsively-you-scroll-matters-more-than-how-long/); [Reason critique](https://reason.com/2025/12/31/study-short-form-video-isnt-rotting-your-brain)
- Media framing such as "TikTok can cause brain rot" overstates what the design can establish. — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/culture/2025/11/24/tiktok-scrolling-can-cause-brain-rot-according-to-new-american-psychological-association-s)

### Inferences
- A product can credibly say that compulsive short-form use is **associated** with worse attention and that reducing compulsive use is a reasonable goal. It should not claim that short-form video "damages" or "rewires" attention.
- Reverse causation is plausible: people with ADHD or poor inhibitory control may gravitate toward short-form feeds. This makes short-form apps natural targets for friction.

### Gaps
- No RCT exposes users to short-form video and measures attention over time.
- No ADHD-specific short-form study was located.

## 6. Digital therapeutics precedent: EndeavorRx, CBT apps, Inflow

### Takeaway
EndeavorRx set the regulatory precedent. It was FDA De Novo authorized (2020) on a single industry-sponsored RCT showing a small improvement on a computerized attention test, without clear gains on clinician-rated ADHD symptoms, and it then **failed commercially**. Meta-analyses put digital ADHD interventions at **small effects (SMD/g ≈ −0.32 to −0.33)**, with nothing for hyperactivity. CBT apps for adults (Inflow, attexis) have pilot or waitlist-controlled data and one large preprint RCT.

### Cited Findings
- **[RCT, COI] STARS-ADHD (Kollins et al., Lancet Digital Health 2020):** n = 348 children aged 8–12, double-blind, digital control. TOVA API mean change was 0.93 versus 0.03 (median difference 0.88, 95% CI 0.24–1.49, p = 0.006). There were no serious adverse events; frustration (3%) and headache (2%) were reported. Akili sponsored the trial. — [DOAJ abstract](https://doaj.org/article/22f93deccb38425aa859a9f4892c3a35); [PubMed 33334505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33334505/)
- **FDA De Novo DEN200026 (June 15, 2020):** the device improves attentional function, measured by computer-based testing, in children aged 8–12 with inattentive or combined ADHD. — [Innolitics mirror](https://fda.innolitics.com/device/DEN200026); [FDA review PDF archive](https://public4.pagefreezer.com:443/content/FDA/01-01-2022T01:12/https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN200026.pdf)
- Psychiatric News reported **no significant differences on the ADHD-RS or CGI** in STARS and warned that FDA marketing benchmarks are not the same as clinically important change. The FDA-summary mirror says the secondary endpoints favored EndeavorRx, so the two sources conflict. — [Psychiatric News](https://psychnews.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.pn.2020.8a5)
- **[PILOT, COI] AKL-T01 single-arm trials in adolescents and adults (npj Mental Health Research 2024):** TOVA ACS improved by +2.6 in adolescents and +6.5 in adults. These trials were uncontrolled. — [PubMed 38898133](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38898133/)
- **Commercial outcome:** 2023 revenue was $1.7M ($523k from EndeavorRx, $1.2M from EndeavorOTC). Payers would not cover it, Akili cut 46% of staff in April 2024, and it was acquired by Virtual Therapeutics for about $34M (announced May 2024). — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2024/05/29/virtual-therapeutics-acquire-akili-video-games-adhd/); [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/akili-sell-34m-virtual-therapeutics-digital/717576/)
- **[MA] J Affect Disord 2024:** RCTs with 1,780 cases showed digital interventions reduced overall ADHD symptoms, SMD −0.33. — [PubMed 39191306](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39191306/)
- **[MA] Kasturiratna, Hartanto et al. 2025:** 23 RCTs, 1,472 participants, overall g = −0.32, with **no significant effect on hyperactivity/impulsivity**. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958825001186); [SMU](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/soss_research/4200/)
- **[Umbrella review] Gabarron et al., BMC Psychiatry 2025** (26 systematic reviews) found mixed results: some positive, some neutral or marginal. — [d-nb PDF](https://d-nb.info/1370098391/34)
- **[Report] Strathclyde 2024 (UK adults)** found 13 peer-reviewed interventions and 10 commercial apps, with limited wearable evidence. — [Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/89559/)
- **[RCT preprint, 2025] attexis** (self-guided CBT app; n = 337 adults with confirmed ADHD) is described by its authors as the largest adult digital ADHD RCT to date. It is not yet peer reviewed. — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.27.25334574v1.full.pdf)
- **Inflow:** the open study is described in section 3. A reported 8-week RCT (n = 154, waitlist control) found symptom reductions, but the waitlist design inflates effects and the primary paper is unverified. Inflow raised $11M in 2023. — [Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/?p=97474); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/11/inflow-a-platform-for-managing-adhd-through-cbt-raises-11m)

### Inferences
- An FDA clearance did not equal clinically meaningful symptom change or a viable business. The precedent argues **against making medical or treatment claims** without a payer pathway, and for positioning as a wellness or skills tool.
- The realistic ceiling for an app is a **small effect on inattention** (about 0.3 SD), with little effect on hyperactivity. Marketing claims beyond that are hype.
- Effects measured against a waitlist are roughly 2–3 times those measured against an active control, judging from the CBT data. Discount waitlist-controlled app claims accordingly.

### Gaps
- No 12-month outcome data for any ADHD app.
- The Inflow RCT primary publication was not found.
- Peer-review status of attexis as of Nov 2026 was not verified.

## 7. AI coaches and LLM assistants for executive function

### Takeaway
I found **no controlled trial** of an LLM assistant improving executive function or ADHD outcomes. Existing work consists of expert ratings of ChatGPT-generated plans, a single-user report, design papers and advocacy pieces. Any "AI coach proven to help ADHD" claim is currently hype.

### Cited Findings
- **[Expert-rating study] Daho & Caci (BMC Psychology 2025):** experts judged ChatGPT-generated executive function rehabilitation plans for hypothetical ADHD profiles consistent with established models. No patients were involved. — [Springer s12207](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12207-025-09538-7); [IRIS Unipa](https://iris.unipa.it/handle/10447/698689)
- **[Case report] Archives of Neuroscience 2025:** a single student with ADHD reported better concentration using ChatGPT. — [Brieflands](https://brieflands.com/journals/ans/articles/154348)
- **[Design paper] "Tether" (arXiv 2025):** an LLM desktop assistant for software engineers with ADHD, targeting sustained attention and task initiation. No outcome data. — [arXiv 2509.01946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.01946)
- **[ANEC] CHADD (Dec 2024):** coaches describe AI as a scaffold that reduces working-memory load. No measured outcomes. — [CHADD](https://chadd.org/harnessing-artificial-intelligence-to-live-better-with-adhd/)
- **[Pilot]** The AI body double condition in the 2025 VR study (n = 12) is preliminary. — [arXiv 2509.12153](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12153v1)
- **[Review] "Future of ADHD Care: Evaluating the Efficacy of ChatGPT in Therapy Enhancement."** Speculative. — [PMC10970191](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10970191/)

### Inferences
- The most defensible LLM uses map onto **evidence-backed mechanisms**: breaking tasks into first steps (CBT compensatory skills), drafting if-then plans (implementation intentions), and providing an immediate, low-friction start (delay aversion). Position AI as delivering those techniques, not as an independently proven therapy.
- Risks to design against include over-reliance, open-ended chat that becomes its own distraction, and hallucinated advice. These are inferred, not studied.

### Gaps
- No RCT, retention or harm data on LLM executive function coaches in ADHD.

### Cross-cutting summary for product team

| Feature | Evidence status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Short friction plus easy "dismiss" before distracting apps | **Backed** (PNAS 2023 field/experiment; CHI 2024; general population; developer-authored) | The dismiss option did most of the work; the deliberation message was null |
| Pre-committed, costly-to-override limits | **Backed** (Allcott et al. RCT, n ≈ 2,000) | Not ADHD-specific |
| If-then plans (implementation intentions) | **Promising** (ADHD lab studies, one group) | Cheap to build |
| Immediate, frequent small rewards | **Mechanism backed** (MA on delay aversion) | App-level trials lacking |
| Visual time cues / countdowns | **Mechanism backed** (MA on timing deficits) | No intervention trials |
| CBT-style task breakdown | **Backed as part of CBT** (MA, SMD 0.43 vs active control) | Not tested as a standalone feature |
| Retrieval practice plus spacing | **Backed in general**; ADHD benefit similar but does not fix encoding | Add encoding scaffolds |
| Pomodoro (rigid) | **Mixed/weak** | Make it flexible |
