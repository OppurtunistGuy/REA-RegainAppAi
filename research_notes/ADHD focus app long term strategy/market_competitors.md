# Market and Competitive Landscape: Screen-Time Blockers, Focus Apps and ADHD Productivity Apps (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: compiled from WebSearch results (Nov 2026). Direct fetches to pitchbook.com, entrepreneur.com, play.google.com and apps.apple.com failed with DNS errors in this environment, so store-listing figures come from search snippets and third-party mirrors (AppBrain, Adapty, Tracxn, Latka). Nearly all revenue figures for private apps are third-party estimates, not company disclosures, and should be treated as order-of-magnitude.

## 1. Market size, growth, and ADHD prevalence

### Takeaway
Analyst estimates for "digital detox / digital wellbeing apps" and "ADHD apps" are each roughly $0.5B–$3B in 2025, growing about 11–24% CAGR. Definitions vary so much that no single figure is reliable. The demand side is stronger evidence: about 15.5M US adults have a current ADHD diagnosis, and more than half were diagnosed as adults. In India, pooled child prevalence is about 6–7%, and adult screening studies find 5–26%, yet diagnosis and support infrastructure are thin.

**ADHD prevalence and diagnosis: US**
- 6.0% of US adults (1 in 16, about 15.5M) had a current ADHD diagnosis. Data comes from the NCHS Rapid Surveys System, Oct–Nov 2023, with 7,046 adults; diagnoses were self-reported — [CDC MMWR 73(40), Oct 2024](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7340a1.htm)
- 55.9% of adults with ADHD were diagnosed in adulthood (age 18 or older) — [CDC MMWR](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7340a1.htm)
- About one-third of adults with ADHD took a stimulant in the past year, and 71.5% of those had trouble filling the prescription because of shortages. About half have used telehealth for ADHD services — [CDC MMWR](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7340a1.htm)
- National adult prevalence estimates had not been available since 2003 — [CDC MMWR](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7340a1.htm)
- An NCHS data brief (Dec 2025) found that 41.7% of health-center visits by adults with ADHD had a documented amphetamine prescription — [NCHS Data Brief 543](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/databriefs/db543.pdf)

**ADHD prevalence: India**
- ICMR-funded meta-analysis (Chauhan et al., 2022, Indian J Pediatrics) of 19 studies: pooled prevalence of 63.2 per 1,000 children (about 6.3%; 95% CI 49.2–77.1). Community-based settings showed 18.6 per 1,000; school-based settings showed 75.1 per 1,000 (ages 4–19) — [Springer, Indian J Pediatr](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12098-021-03999-9)
- A later review cites the Indian pooled figure as 7.1% — [Frontiers in Psychiatry 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1646618/full)
- Adult ADHD in India: a systematic review found prevalence of 5.48%–25.7% across general and specific populations. A Delhi-NCR study of 1,665 college-age young adults found 14% screened positive (a screen, not a diagnosis) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9093659); [PMC12435561](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12435561)
- A 2025 paper on children with ADHD in India calls for strengthening diagnosis, support, training and research, which signals underdiagnosis and capacity gaps — [Univ. of Greenwich repository, 2025](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/48880/9/48880%20MORGAN_Children_With_ADHD_In_India_Strengthening_Diagnosis_Support_Training_And_Research_%28OA%29_2025.pdf)
- Global: a pre-2023 systematic review and meta-analysis of ADHD prevalence (older data) — [PubMed 25733754](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25733754/)

### Inferences
- Analyst market sizes are not reliable enough for bottom-up planning. Their 2025 bases differ about 5x and the CAGRs are mutually inconsistent. A credible reading is that ADHD-specific consumer app spend today is probably low hundreds of millions of dollars, with a few winners (Tiimo, Opal, Forest, Brain.fm) each at single-digit to low tens of millions ARR. Akili's tiny revenue shows that "ADHD apps" priced as therapeutics have not monetized.
- Demand tailwind: adult diagnosis is rising and more than half of US adult diagnoses happen in adulthood. Stimulant shortages push people toward non-drug coping tools.
- India: applying about 6–7% child prevalence to roughly 440M+ under-18s implies tens of millions of affected children, most of them undiagnosed. The population figure is general knowledge, not sourced here, and this is an inference, not a sourced figure. A "self-identified distractibility / focus for students" framing likely reaches more of the India market than a clinical "ADHD" label.

### Gaps
- No WHO global adult ADHD figure was retrieved in this session. Commonly cited estimates (~2.5–3% persistent adult ADHD worldwide) were not verified here.
- No official ICMR national adult ADHD prevalence exists; only regional and screening studies were found.
- No data.ai or Sensor Tower public total for "screen time / focus" category revenue was found.
- No US prescription or diagnosis growth trend data for 2023–2026 was found (e.g., Epic Research / IQVIA stimulant script growth).

## 2. Key players: downloads, ratings, revenue, funding, pricing

### Takeaway
The category splits into (a) VC-light or indie blockers with strong ratings and modest revenue (one sec, ScreenZen, Jomo, AppBlock), (b) a few venture-backed scale players (Opal, Tiimo, Inflow, Flow Club), (c) hardware "friction" devices (Brick, Unpluq), and (d) focus-audio and body-doubling (Brain.fm, Endel, Focusmate, Flow Club). Regain is an Indian, Android-heavy entrant with about 5M+ Play downloads and a top-25 India Productivity ranking, but it has low iOS traction and weak monetization rank.

### Inferences
- The tier of category leaders by revenue appears to be Opal (about $5–10M ARR est.), Forest, Brain.fm and Tiimo (Tiimo's App of the Year likely boosted 2026 revenue). One sec, ScreenZen, Jomo and AppBlock are indie-scale, which shows blocking alone is easily copied and hard to monetize at scale.
- Regain's profile (5M+ downloads, #24 free but #132 grossing in India) points to strong acquisition but weak monetization. That pattern is typical of India, where ARPU is low.
- Physical friction (Brick, Unpluq) is the fastest-growing new format in 2025–26. Clones on Amazon (about 400K units per year across listings) show demand, and also that hardware is copied quickly.
- Body doubling (Focusmate, Flow Club) and visual time-planning (Tiimo) are the ADHD-native mechanics with the strongest product–need fit. Blockers are not ADHD-specific.

## 3. Sustainable growth vs. churn or shutdowns; acquisitions

### Takeaway
Sustainable winners are mostly lean, consumer-paid and design-led (one sec, ScreenZen, Forest, Tiimo, Brain.fm). The large failures and write-downs are in the medicalized ADHD segment: Akili (FDA-cleared game, $1B SPAC to a $34M sale) and Cerebral (telehealth stimulant-prescribing scandal and layoffs).

### Cited Findings
- Akili Interactive (EndeavorRx, first FDA-cleared ADHD video game, 2020) went public in 2022 at about a $1B valuation. In May 2024 it agreed to be acquired by Virtual Therapeutics for about $34M (43.4 cents per share, a 4% premium). 2023 revenue was $1.7M — [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/akili-sell-34m-virtual-therapeutics-digital/717576/); [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2024/05/29/virtual-therapeutics-acquire-akili-video-games-adhd/); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/adhd-game-developer-akili-interactive-be-taken-private-after-being-sold-34m?utm_id=55)
- Cerebral stopped new ADHD stimulant prescriptions in 2022 under federal scrutiny. It laid off about 20% of staff in Oct 2022 and later about 15% more, and its CEO stepped down in Dec 2024. CVS stopped filling controlled-substance scripts from Cerebral and Done Health providers — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/cerebral-announces-layoffs-part-operational-restructure-amid-federal-investigation); [Behavioral Health Business](https://bhbusiness.com/2022/10/24/troubled-digital-mental-health-startup-cerebral-lays-off-more-than-1000-employees/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_(company)); [Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2022/05/05/310299.htm)
- One sec moved from one-time purchase to subscription for sustainability and remains self-funded — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/businesses/one-sec); [press kit](https://one-sec.pressdeck.io/)
- Forest switched from paid upfront to freemium subscription (Dec 2025) after its long-run peak — [Makerstack](https://makerstack.co/reviews/forest-review/)
- Jomo's "Jomo needs you!" page signals sustainability strain for a self-funded indie blocker — [Jomo help](https://help.jomo.so/en/article/jomo-needs-you-1fq5ura/)
- Opal publicly joked about a Meta acquisition (April Fools 2025); no real acquisition — [SpeedBump](https://speedbumpapp.com/en/blog/opal-bought-by-meta/)

### Inferences
- The "prescription digital therapeutic" route (FDA clearance plus payer reimbursement) has been commercially punishing for ADHD. A consumer-paid wellness or learning positioning with optional evidence (one sec's PNAS study is a good model) is the lower-risk path.
- Recurring pattern: blockers built on OS APIs (Apple Screen Time framework, Android Accessibility) face platform risk and are easy to clone. Lasting players add a distinct mechanic (breath friction, tree gamification, visual planning, hardware) or brand.

### Gaps
- Did not confirm whether the Akili–Virtual Therapeutics deal closed (expected Q3 2024).
- No verified 2023–2026 shutdown list of consumer focus/ADHD apps was found (e.g., any Flown, Done, Inflow pivots). Done Health's status was not established.

## 4. Business models and conversion/retention benchmarks

### Takeaway
Subscription is the dominant model (annual $20–$100). Lifetime options and student discounts are common, and tip-jar or free models exist but stay small. RevenueCat data shows Health & Fitness has the highest trial-to-paid and LTV of any category, but also high refund rates and heavy monthly-plan mix. B2B, school and insurance channels exist mainly in the medicalized segment and have underperformed.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025 (75K+ apps, $10B+ revenue tracked): Health & Fitness median trial-to-paid conversion 39.9% (top 10%: 68.3%); highest median 14-day ARPU $0.44 (upper quartile $1.31); highest payer LTV, median $16.44 (upper quartile $31.12). Education (4.86%) and H&F (4.71%) have the highest refund rates — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [RevenueCat launch post](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/company/the-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-launch)
- Trials of 17–32 days had the highest median conversion, 45.7% — [Singular summary of RevenueCat](https://www.singular.net/blog/subscription-apps/)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026: H&F leads yearly realized LTV at $35.64 median (more than 3x Gaming's $11.22); H&F is 68% monthly-plan; offer usage overall is 9.3%, with H&F highest at 14%. "Top performers convert over 23%" (likely a different metric, e.g., download-to-paid) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- Fitness apps are highly monetizable but winner-take-most — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/fitness-apps-monetizable-winner-take-all-or-most/)
- Observed price points: Opal $99/yr; Brain.fm $99.99/yr; Tiimo about $54/yr; one sec $19.99/yr ($9.99 student, $39.99–$99.99 lifetime); Goblin Tools $29.99/$64.99 Pro; Forest Plus from $5.99/mo; ScreenZen tips only; Brick about $59 one-time hardware (sources in section 2)
- Medicalized model: EndeavorRx (prescription) generated only $523K and EndeavorOTC $1.2M in 2023 — [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/akili-sell-34m-virtual-therapeutics-digital/717576/)

### Inferences
- For India, $99/yr US-style pricing will not convert at scale. Regain's low grossing rank fits this. Likely needs: regional pricing (₹ annual plans, UPI autopay), student plans, and possibly B2B2C via coaching institutes, schools and edtech, where parents pay.
- A hybrid works well for defensibility: a cheap or free blocker as acquisition, and paid value in ADHD-specific coaching, planning or learning features plus a lifetime option for subscription-averse ADHD users (one sec and Goblin Tools show demand for one-time pricing).

### Gaps
- No public benchmark for day-30 / month-12 retention specific to focus or ADHD apps. RevenueCat retention renewal percentages were not captured in snippets.
- No data on employer or insurer reimbursement for consumer ADHD apps outside the DTx segment; no India-specific conversion benchmarks found.

## 5. White-spaces

### Takeaway
No one clearly owns "ADHD and distraction-free learning for students," especially in India and emerging markets. Western ADHD tools (Tiimo, Inflow, Focusmate) are English-first, priced in USD and planner- or therapy-oriented. Blockers (Opal, one sec, Regain) are not ADHD-specific and not learning-integrated.

### Cited Findings
- India child ADHD prevalence is about 6.3–7.1%, and school-based prevalence (75 per 1,000) is far higher than community-based (18.6 per 1,000), which suggests schools are where it gets detected — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12098-021-03999-9)
- 14% of Delhi-NCR college-age young adults screened positive for ADHD — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9093659)
- A 2025 paper calls for strengthening diagnosis and support for children with ADHD in India — [Greenwich 2025](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/48880/9/48880%20MORGAN_Children_With_ADHD_In_India_Strengthening_Diagnosis_Support_Training_And_Research_%28OA%29_2025.pdf)
- Regain already ranks #24 in India free Productivity and blocks Reels/Shorts, with "Study Timer" positioning — [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/regain-screen-time-focus/ai.regainapp)
- US adults: 55.9% diagnosed in adulthood, and stimulant shortages are widespread, pointing to demand for non-drug self-management — [CDC MMWR](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7340a1.htm)
- Opal is reported as "great for phone, weak on Mac", so cross-device enforcement is an acknowledged weakness — [tryhugo Opal review 2026](https://www.tryhugo.app/blog/opal-app-review-2026)
- Physical friction devices are a growing segment (Brick plus Amazon clones about $6.4M per 12 months) — [AsinSight](https://www.asinsight.com/market-analysis/US/brick-phone-blocker)

### Inferences
- **ADHD + learning:** combine blocking with study scaffolding, such as task breakdown (Goblin Tools-style), visual time (Tiimo-style), body doubling (Focusmate/Flow Club-style rooms of fellow students) and spaced or short-session learning. No observed player integrates all of these.
- **India and emerging-market students:** board/JEE/NEET exam prep, vernacular languages, parent-visible progress (without surveillance), low-end Android, and UPI micro-pricing. Distribution through coaching institutes, schools and edtech partners could be the moat that global apps lack.
- **Cross-device:** phone plus laptop (Chrome/Mac/Windows) enforcement for studying. Opal is weak on Mac and Regain is mobile-only.
- **Defensibility levers:** longitudinal focus/attention data and personalization, community or accountability networks (network effects), institutional (B2B2C) contracts and a published efficacy study. Blocker features alone are easily copied (ScreenZen gives them away for free).

### Gaps
- No data found on Indian edtech or school buyers' willingness to pay for focus/ADHD tools.
- No competitor found that is explicitly ADHD + student-learning focused in India. That absence could reflect search limits rather than a true market gap.
